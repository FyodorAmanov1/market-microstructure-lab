# MM06: Queue-aware fill analysis

## Question

How much completion is lost when a passive order repeatedly cancels and rejoins
at an unchanged price, relative to holding its FIFO priority? Measure queue
position and time-to-fill on recorded orders, including partial fills and
nonfills, rather than drawing independent fill outcomes.

## Method

`QueueFillBook` extends the existing public `LimitOrderBook` without changing its
matching code. Call `advance(timestamp)` before accepted commands to set a
nondecreasing integer exchange clock; equal timestamps retain call order. Every
accepted order records its ID, acceptance time, original quantity, initial
resting queue position, first/full execution timestamps, cancellation time and
cancelled quantity. Executed quantities are attributed through the maker and
taker IDs of actual `Trade` records, including incoming partial fills that later
rest and execute as makers. No independent fill draws participate.

`position(order_id)` measures one-based rank and remaining units ahead at the
same price, plus separate better-priced volume. Fully executed or cancelled
orders have no live position. Cancelling another order removes its remaining
quantity without reordering survivors; a new submission receives a new ID.
The [Nasdaq OUCH overview](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/Trader.aspx?id=OUCH)
provides a primary price-time matching reference. This implementation does not
claim Nasdaq conformance or reproduce venue-specific amendment exceptions.

The applied case runs 32 paired seeds, 600 through 631. Each seed generates
eight fresh-book episodes, alternating buy and sell, with identical exogenous
commands for both policies. Initial depth is two passive background orders,
each uniformly 3..8 units. The agent joins with four units at 99 for buys or
101 for sells, around a fixed reference midpoint of 100. Depth persists within
each episode; there is no periodic background reset. Both policies receive
the same 20-event horizon. Hold keeps its initial order. Rejoin cancels its
remaining child and submits only the parent's remaining quantity at the same
price at event times 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20, before that event's aggressive flow.
Completion on event 20 counts; otherwise the residual remains open. Rejoining
at the deadline can create a zero-age open child, which remains in the ledger.

Each event processes a background join (probability 0.7, uniform size 1..4),
cancellation of the oldest live background order (probability 0.12), policy
re-entry if due, and opposite IOC flow (probability 0.55, uniform size 1..4).
These probabilities generate commands, never individual order fill outcomes.
IOC remainders are cancelled. Background cancellation targets may diverge after
policy-dependent fills, although the cancellation rule and draws are shared.

Child first/full waits are execution time minus acceptance time. Full-fill wait
is missing unless the entire original child quantity executes; cancellation
does not complete an order. Open lifetimes are administratively censored at
the observation deadline. Cancelled lifetimes end at cancellation and remain
a separate competing outcome. No independent-censoring assumption is imposed.
Parent first/full waits start at time zero across all replacement children.
Restricted parent completion time is `min(full fill time, 20)`, taking 20 for
unfinished parents; it includes all parents and is not an extrapolated mean
time until eventual completion. Conditional fill-time means describe only
the observed subset and can be selection biased.

Paired differences first average eight episodes within each seed. Pointwise
95% Student-t intervals use the 32 seed differences and 31 degrees of freedom,
following the existing [NIST paired-observation method](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section3/prc312.htm).
There is no multiplicity correction or historical-data inference.

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Parents | 256 | 256 |
| Fully completed parents | 253 | 54 |
| Parents with any observed fill | 255 | 96 |
| Total executed units | 1,014 | 298 |
| Mean filled units per four-unit parent | 3.960938 | 1.164062 |
| Mean restricted completion time / events | 9.335938 | 17.937500 |
| Conditional mean first-fill time / events | 7.274510 | 8.156250 |
| Conditional mean full-fill time / events | 9.209486 | 10.222222 |
| Accepted child orders | 256 | 1,374 |
| Cancelled children | 0 | 1,118 |
| Deadline-open children | 3 | 202 |

Rejoin minus hold mean filled quantity is **-2.796875 units**, with paired 95%
interval **[-3.005687, -2.588063]**. Completion-rate difference is -0.777344
([-0.821284, -0.733404]); restricted completion-time difference is +8.601562
events ([8.093946, 9.109179]). Repeated re-entry loses progress even when both
policies quote the same price. The conditional completion means alone would
substantially conceal the difference because most re-entry parents never finish.

The illustrative buy starts third in line behind eight units. Hold completes;
the re-entry parent gets no fills. Its six successive children are five
cancellations and one deadline-open order. That one episode is illustrative,
not the aggregate outcome. All 1,630 children retain their actual observed
outcomes; partial and unsuccessful parents are not removed.

Agent fees are 0.05 tick per executed unit: mean fees per parent are 0.198047
under hold and 0.058203 under re-entry. Lower re-entry fees reflect fewer fills,
not an execution advantage. Gross book cash and inventory conserve after each
event. Fees are an external sink; funding and borrow charges are zero. Executed
inventory is retained, and replay marked equity uses midpoint 100 after fees.
No terminal liquidation, arrival-shortfall or profitability ranking is claimed
for this queue experiment; incomplete quantity is not executed inventory.

All 54 tests pass. Seven added tests cover hand-computed queue depletion on
both sides, better-priced volume, cancellation/partial-fill priority, zero-wait
taker fills and later maker fills, cancelled versus open lifetimes, tied-time
FIFO, atomic failures, observation versus plain-book ledger equality over 150
commands, parent deadline accounting, paired aggregation, and exact CSV
agreement. Existing engine fingerprint and matching-oracle checks still pass.

## Limitations

All prices, orders and timestamps are synthetic. Fixed prices deliberately
isolate queue priority: this design omits the potential benefit of refreshing
a stale price, adverse selection and changing market conditions. It does not
establish that holding orders is a good live strategy. Depth is finite and
persistent only within independent episodes, with exogenous flow and a stylized
background cancellation rule. There are no hidden orders, auctions, network
delay, credit limits, calibrated impact or venue-specific priority exceptions.
Time is abstract event time, not seconds. This timestamped recorder is separate
from the latency-message adapter and does not infer missing timestamps from
the earlier accepted-command JSON format. Legacy public quote-arrival metrics
are preserved as a separate model and are not evidence for these queue results.

Intervals describe Monte Carlo variation under these chosen assumptions, not
model error or real-market fill prediction. Cancellation is informative, so
cancelled children cannot be treated as ordinary independent right-censoring
to estimate hypothetical eventual fills. No such survival extrapolation is
performed. Matching observation equivalence checks internal behavior, not
independent exchange validity. No novelty or market edge is claimed.

## What the author should understand for an interview

Trace the distinction between same-price rank, remaining volume ahead and
better-priced volume. Explain how a partial maker fill preserves priority and
how cancellation followed by submission changes ID, rank and child age.
Distinguish child completion from parent completion across replacements, and
first fill from full fill. Explain why blank waits are not zeros, why a
cancelled order is not complete, and why conditioning on completed parents can
hide a poor completion rate. Derive the restricted completion-time measure
and explain its deadline dependence. Justify pairing by seed, keep fees tied
to actual filled units, and distinguish cash from retained inventory. Finally,
explain why this fixed-price result says nothing about the benefit of repricing
in a moving market or the validity of the synthetic flow assumptions.

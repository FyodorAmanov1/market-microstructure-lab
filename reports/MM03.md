# Deterministic latency queue

## Question

With order-submission delay held fixed, does slower cancellation increase cancellation-pending fills and reduce liquidated net value? The baseline cancels immediately; the comparison delays cancellation by six abstract time units. Both submit replacements after two units and use the same market paths. This studies an observable message race, rather than approximating latency by refreshing quotes less often.

## Method

`src/latency.py` wraps the existing price-time book with immutable message records. Each submission has a client ID, send timestamp and arrival timestamp. A heap processes messages by `(arrival timestamp, enqueue sequence)`; tied cancels receive no special precedence. Exchange order IDs and FIFO positions are assigned on arrival. A cancel targets the original client ID and removes only the remaining quantity of that order. Other orders retain priority. Requests with invalid timestamps or ownership are rejected before changing the queue.

An overtaking cancellation misses if its submission has not arrived. It does not suppress the future order; a retry is required. Cancels after complete fills also miss. Receipts distinguish successful cancellation quantities from misses. Every fill records its exchange timestamp and any client orders whose cancellation had already been requested. Stale units count these executed quantities, including partial fills, exactly once per participating client order. Stale means cancellation intent, not necessarily an unfavorable price. Stale midpoint markout is signed quantity times `(current midpoint - fill price)`; positive values are favorable to the agent. It is an attribution measure already reflected in the ledger, not an extra charge or causal loss estimate.

Each 120-batch synthetic path uses ten integer time units per batch. Background quotes refresh at offset 0; the agent requests cancellation and replacement at 1; replacements arrive at 3; external marketable flow arrives at 5; delayed cancellation arrives at 7. Quotes use inventory skew 0.35, at most three new units per side. Outstanding orders reserve risk capacity until their cancels arrive, keeping inventory within +/-20 units. Delayed cancellations therefore overlap old and new quotes, and reservation can reduce replacement size. This is the total outcome of that specified policy, not a controlled comparison with identical displayed liquidity.

Five background levels per side contain ten units each at midpoint +/-3 through +/-7. Depth is finite within a batch and fully replenished next batch. Fees are 0.05 tick per executed unit, including liquidation. Funding, borrow charges and calibrated impact are zero. At the end, all messages have arrived, outstanding orders are removed, and inventory liquidates through five refreshed finite levels. Marked equity, net cash after liquidation, fees and liquidation cost are reported separately. Aggregate pre-fee cash and inventory conserve throughout; fees are an external sink.

Sixteen fresh paired seeds 300 through 315 share pre-generated prices, flow directions and sizes. Student-t 95% intervals use the sample standard deviation of within-seed net differences with 15 degrees of freedom. Replay seed 1729 is separate. The raw artifact retains per-seed scalar ledgers, recorded replay paths and message/fill receipts for the first two replay batches; the queue API exposes full receipts during a run.

## Actual findings

All values below are means over sixteen paired synthetic paths. Net and markout are in tick-value units; stale fills are quantity units.

| Regime | Immediate net | Delayed net | Delayed minus immediate | Paired 95% interval | Delayed stale units | Delayed stale midpoint markout |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Balanced | 277.81875 | 360.88750 | 83.06875 | [52.87179, 113.26571] | 153.1875 | 209.2500 |
| Rising | 317.27500 | 325.05625 | 7.78125 | [-41.54661, 57.10911] | 147.3750 | 176.3125 |

Immediate cancellation produces zero stale executed units in both regimes. Delayed cancellation produces substantial exposure, but its mean midpoint markout is positive. The hypothesis that delayed cancellation necessarily reduces net value is not supported here. Overlapping quotes provide extra opportunities to capture the modeled spread. The rising-case interval includes zero, so the experiment does not resolve that policy difference.

Balanced mean fees rise from 10.49375 to 15.61250 ticks; mean liquidation cost rises from 6.10000 to 14.48750. Rising mean fees rise from 10.60000 to 15.31875, while liquidation cost rises from 8.00625 to 12.009375. Those actual costs are included in the net comparison. Every paired run records zero ledger violations and finishes with zero inventory.

In the separate balanced replay, immediate and delayed net are 291.40 and 381.65 ticks; delayed cancellation produces 143 stale units, 43 missed cancels and maximum absolute inventory 12. In the rising replay, the corresponding net values are 308.80 and 371.45, with 176 stale units, 56 missed cancels and maximum inventory 15. Missed cancels are expected when their targets finish before the cancel arrives; they are not dropped accounting events.

## Limitations and reference boundary

All data are synthetic. Integer times have no calibrated conversion to exchange clock time. Delays are fixed with no jitter, packet loss, acknowledgement lag or endogenous market reaction. Background replenishment and spread-capturing flow favor extra resting quotes; the test does not establish a live trading edge or prove slow cancellation beneficial. The model includes full cancellation only, allows self-trades in the underlying book and omits hidden liquidity, auctions and exchange-specific order types. Stale classification is request-based and persists after an overtaking cancel misses until an explicit retry removes the order.

[Nasdaq's OUCH specification](https://nasdaqtrader.com/content/technicalsupport/specifications/TradingProducts/OUCH4.2.pdf) documents timestamped execution/cancellation responses and cancellation rejection. Its [official overview](https://classic.nasdaqtrader.com/Trader.aspx?id=ouch) describes price-time matching. These are protocol references, not a claim that this simulator reproduces Nasdaq. The arrival/sequence tie rule and overtaking-cancel behavior are explicit simulator conventions.

## What the author should understand for an interview

Trace a cancel/fill race with tied timestamps and explain why enqueue order decides it. Distinguish send time, arrival time, client ID and exchange FIFO ID. Explain why partial fills retain priority, why removing a middle order cannot reorder survivors, and why pending cancellation cannot free inventory capacity early. Reconcile fill cash, signed inventory, external fees and finite-depth liquidation. Explain why a stale fill can have positive markout, why that markout must not be added again to net value, and why the positive synthetic latency result depends on overlap, replenishment and external flow assumptions. Separate the replay example from paired uncertainty and the legacy refresh-cadence comparison from actual message delay.

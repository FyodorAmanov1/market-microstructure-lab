# Finite-depth TWAP execution

## Question

Does spreading a parent buy over equal time intervals reduce arrival-price implementation shortfall relative to executing as early as finite depth permits? What happens when the parent exceeds available liquidity? The comparison measures actual matched trades, including incomplete execution, instead of assuming every unit fills at the touch.

## Method

The applied execution engine reuses the studio's price–time book and owner-checked cancellation. An aggressive child sets its limit to the worst displayed opposite price, matches resting orders in price/FIFO order and immediately cancels its unfilled remainder. There is no fabricated depth or residual resting parent order. TWAP releases cumulative targets `floor(Q*(t+1)/T)` at each of twelve batches, carrying any deficit into the next child. The baseline, Sweep + retry, releases the entire parent at batch zero and retries residual units at each subsequent batch until the same deadline. Both policies can finish early or remain incomplete.

Each batch first refreshes background liquidity, then processes external aggressive flow, then the parent child. Background liquidity comprises five levels per side, ten units per level, three through seven ticks from the reference midpoint. External flow has equiprobable buy/sell sides and uniform integer size 1–8. Reference increments are uniform integers -2 through +2; the rising case adds an independent Bernoulli(0.6) increment. Arrival is the initial 10,000-tick reference midpoint before the first external event. All paths are synthetic and generated before either policy runs, using NumPy's seeded generator. Policies cannot alter external draws; they do alter their own remaining displayed depth within each batch.

The 120-unit balanced and rising cases and 700-unit capacity case use the same twelve-batch horizon. Seed 1729 supplies the illustrative replay and full child/fill ledger. Sixteen independent paired seeds 200–215 supply means and differences. A paired 95% Student t interval uses 15 degrees of freedom and `mean(d) +/- 2.13145*std(d, ddof=1)/sqrt(16)` for TWAP minus baseline. The cases were chosen to show timing risk and capacity limits, not selected for favorable performance. The intervals are exploratory, conditional on these synthetic assumptions, without adjustment across scenarios.

Let `s=+1` for a buy and `-1` for a sell, `F` be actual filled units, `N` executed notional, `R=Q-F` unexecuted units, `a` arrival midpoint and `mT` terminal reference midpoint. Filled-order shortfall is `s*(N-F*a)`. Fees are `0.05*F` ticks, charged only on actual fills. Unexecuted opportunity cost is `s*R*(mT-a)`. Total arrival-price implementation shortfall is their sum; positive values represent cost. This equals the terminal wealth of the hypothetical full parent filled at arrival minus the actual account wealth including fees. The identity is tested for buys and sells. VWAP is `N/F`, or null when nothing executes. Cash before fees and signed executed inventory reconcile exactly with book trades; fees are a separate external sink. The unexecuted parent is reported as positive remaining units, separately from the signed inventory actually traded. Funding and borrow costs are zero.

## Actual findings

Means over sixteen paired seeds, in tick-value units except residual quantity:

| Case | Sweep + retry total IS | TWAP total IS | TWAP minus baseline | Paired 95% CI | Mean residual, both |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Balanced, Q=120 | 548.9375 | 389.5000 | -159.4375 | [-313.7141, -5.1609] | 0 |
| Rising, Q=120 | 605.7500 | 745.6875 | 139.9375 | [-14.2235, 294.0985] | 0 |
| Capacity, Q=700 | 2963.29375 | 2963.29375 | 0 | [0, 0] | 124.125 |

Both 120-unit policies fill every unit in every paired path and pay 6 ticks per parent. Their mean filled-order shortfalls are respectively 542.9375 and 383.5 in balanced flow, and 599.75 and 739.6875 in rising flow. Opportunity cost is zero because completion is full. TWAP reduces depth-walking costs in the balanced case under these replenishment assumptions. Rising prices make delay costly: TWAP is worse on average, although that paired interval crosses zero. Preserve this negative result rather than claiming TWAP always improves execution.

In the capacity case both policies consume the entire available ask side every batch: the first TWAP target already exceeds maximum per-batch capacity. Consequently their realized trades are identical, not statistically independent policy outcomes. Each fills 575.875 units on average, pays 28.79375 ticks, incurs 2907.0625 ticks of filled shortfall and 27.4375 ticks of terminal-marked opportunity cost. The zero difference interval reflects identical paths under a binding capacity constraint; it is not evidence of universal equivalence.

The separate recorded seed demonstrates why a replay must not be confused with a mean. Balanced TWAP costs 16 ticks versus 638 for the baseline; rising TWAP costs 520 versus 726, even though its paired mean is worse. In the capacity replay both fill 560 units, leave 140 unexecuted, and pay 28 ticks. Filled shortfall is 1044 ticks and opportunity cost is -560, yielding total shortfall 512. The negative opportunity cost is retained: falling reference prices benefit waiting on a buy, but do not complete it. All recorded execution batches pass cash, inventory, quantity and no-resting-parent checks.

## Limitations

Full background replenishment each batch is a strong, favorable assumption for splitting execution. Depletion is real within each batch but does not persist across batches; reference prices and future flow are exogenous, with no permanent or calibrated temporary impact. There are no historical exchange data, hidden liquidity, auctions, network timestamps, latency queue, credit constraints, fee tiers, funding or borrow charges. A batch is an abstract time interval, not a claim about clock-time execution speed. The parent trades aggressively; passive execution and queue-waiting strategies are not studied.

Residual opportunity cost is a terminal midpoint valuation, not a liquidation trade or guaranteed attainable completion price. No terminal forced fill is added. The study reports the shortfall components so a lower cost cannot conceal unexecuted quantity. Sixteen synthetic pairs give limited uncertainty evidence and do not establish production accuracy, optimality or a market edge. The unchanged legacy quote-arrival results in the top-level artifact remain a separate model.

The execution-risk motivation follows [Almgren and Chriss (2001)](https://doi.org/10.21314/jor.2001.041); this code does not implement their optimal impact-control solution. [Interactive Brokers' primary TWAP documentation](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/docs/general/order-types/algorithmic-orders/ib-algorithms/twap) describes time-based execution, catch-up and possible noncompletion; this synthetic schedule is not a reproduction of that broker's algorithm.

## What the author should understand for an interview

Trace a multi-level child fill and explain why its unfilled IOC remainder is cancelled while previously resting makers retain FIFO priority. Derive the arrival-price shortfall identity and explain the sign change for sells, why fees depend on filled units, and why residual units are not executed inventory. Distinguish the schedule's target from its actual completion path and the replay seed from paired evidence. Explain why common external draws support paired comparisons, why the rising case is an honest negative finding, and why saturated policies coincide. Finally, explain why replenishment assumptions and terminal midpoint marks limit what these costs say about an actual exchange.

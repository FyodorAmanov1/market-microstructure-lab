# MM04: Fresh-seed policy confirmation

## Question

Does the earlier reduction in inventory exposure, accompanied by lower liquidated net P&L, persist when the existing quoting policies face new synthetic paths? The meaningful baseline is symmetric quoting, not a zero-trade account. This confirmation concerns the original three quoting policies; execution schedules and timestamped cancellation policies retain their separate studies.

## Frozen method

The machine-readable [confirmation plan](../docs/confirmation-plan.json) fixes policies, regimes, 240-batch horizon, cost assumptions, outcomes, contrasts, sample size and interpretation before evaluating the confirmation seeds. Symmetric uses skew 0 and refreshes each batch; inventory skew uses 0.35 and refreshes each batch; delayed refresh uses 0.35 and refreshes every fifth batch. There is no parameter search, optional stopping, seed removal or selection of a favorable regime. This is a local reproducible specification, without an external preregistration or timestamp authority.

The plan fingerprints the policy simulator, price-time book, order/trade records and account implementation with SHA-256. Every confirmation run rejects a different engine fingerprint. The output also records a canonical plan digest, making subsequent specification changes visible. The recorded plan digest is `c9b7917b5e2df451faeab440bd4f2285540f7d9674a2b9c2d2bad00d729e5483`.

Exactly 32 new seeds, 400-431, are used in each of balanced, upward-trending and informed-flow regimes: 96 regime/seed comparisons, each with three policies (288 runs). They exclude previous study seeds 100-115, 200-215, 300-315 and illustrative replay seed 1729. Within each seed and regime, policies receive the same pre-generated reference moves, sides, sizes and informed-flow draws. Random draws do not depend on policy fills. Regimes reuse seeds, so their outcomes must not be pooled as independent observations.

The primary outcomes are liquidated net in tick-value units and mean absolute inventory in quantity units. Exposure is `sum(abs(q_t))/240` across all end-of-batch observations before liquidation, including zeros. Terminal inventory after liquidation would conceal exposure; maximum absolute inventory is reported as a secondary diagnostic. Seed-level average exposure captures duration but is not dollar risk, volatility, or a tail-risk bound.

For every policy mean and every policy-minus-symmetric contrast, the interval is `mean +/- t(0.975,31)*sample_sd/sqrt(32)`, using `ddof=1`. Differences are formed within seed before calculating the standard error. Event batches are serially dependent and are not treated as independent samples. The [NIST paired-observation formula](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section3/prc312.htm) is the statistical reference. These are pointwise 95% intervals without multiplicity correction; no simultaneous coverage or universal policy ranking is claimed. The fixed interpretation rule confirms lower net with lower exposure only when both paired upper endpoints are below zero; unresolved and opposite findings would be retained.

Every fill uses the actual finite price-time book. Fees are 0.05 tick per executed unit, including terminal liquidation. Liquidation consumes five refreshed levels of ten units each at terminal reference midpoint plus/minus 3 through 7 ticks. Each policy's position is bounded by 20 units, within liquidation capacity. `liquidation_cost = terminal_marked_equity - liquidated_net` includes liquidation slippage and liquidation fees. It is already reflected in net and must not be subtracted twice. Funding and borrow costs are zero; the fee schedule and full background replenishment are synthetic assumptions.

The negative net result persists despite lower fees and liquidation costs for inventory-managed policies. Lower exposure does not improve the chosen net objective in this simulator. Balanced and trend exposure and costs match exactly for the two policies that refresh each batch in this sample. The trend is an exogenous price drift; their quotes track the current reference, and there is no inventory-sensitive flow response. This illustrates a model boundary rather than independent replication across regimes. Every simulation enforces aggregate cash/inventory conservation after each batch and finishes flat through finite-depth liquidation. The confirmation additionally checks matched reference paths and the marked-minus-liquidation-cost reconciliation.

## Limitations

All events and reference prices are synthetic. Fresh pseudo-random seeds test Monte Carlo stability under fixed assumptions; they do not validate those assumptions against market data. Full background depth replenishment, exogenous informed flow and zero funding/borrow costs shape these results. No permanent impact, calibrated latency, hidden liquidity, exchange-specific fee tiers or changing market participation is modeled. This milestone does not reconfirm TWAP or timestamped cancellation results. The delayed-refresh policy is a cadence experiment.

Student-t coverage is exact under normal independent seed outcomes and approximate otherwise; 32 seeds do not establish normality or bound tail losses. Pointwise intervals are not adjusted across the six policy/regime contrasts or multiple outcomes. Correlated regimes cannot be counted as additional independent evidence. Code fingerprints detect changes relative to the saved specification but do not prove external preregistration, historical immutability or economic realism. These findings establish neither novelty nor a real-market edge.

Trace a fill through cash, signed inventory, fees, marked equity and executable liquidation, explaining why liquidation cost is not deducted twice. Define time-averaged absolute inventory and distinguish it from terminal inventory, peak inventory and risk-adjusted return. Explain what was frozen, how seed disjointness and engine fingerprints are checked, and why changing a specification after observing results requires a new confirmation. Present the lower-net/lower-exposure finding without turning it into a trading recommendation. Explain that an interval spanning zero would mean the comparison is unresolved, not proof of equivalence, and distinguish Monte Carlo uncertainty from model uncertainty.
